      title: "One labor market, two scales of analysis"
      text: |
        **Inside the firm:** hiring, separations, pay, job changes, employee tenure, and decisions made under organizational constraints.

        **Across the economy:** employment, labor turnover, business formation, wage growth, and movement between employers.

        These are connected subjects, but they are **not interchangeable datasets**. Aggregate trends do not automatically identify individual or firm behavior; enterprise data cannot by itself establish economy-wide mechanisms. Much of my research begins by asking where the two perspectives illuminate one another — and where they do not.

        I gravitate toward data about people: what they do, where they work, why they leave, and which constraints shape decisions that look voluntary from a distance. Labor is threaded through household choices, migration, education, health, and nearly every part of economic life. I'm interested in the questions hidden in routine administrative data — particularly when the firm-level and macroeconomic stories seem to disagree.

        Outside work, I enjoy **golf, travel, cooking experiments that cross culinary borders, and studying Turkish**. Living and traveling internationally has made me more curious about which features of work are universal and which depend on institutions, culture, and incentives.

        **Books I return to:** *Mostly Harmless Econometrics*, *The Armchair Economist*, and *Weapons of Math Destruction*. They reflect a preference for careful identification, strong intuition, and a healthy suspicion of tidy explanations.
